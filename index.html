Rules & Points
How a Restaurant Boss grade is earned.
How your grade is built

Judges + customer survey + health points + food cost points + bonus and penalty points = final score

Customers20
Prep time60 min
Rush1 hour
Food budget$140
StaffUp to 5
Food cost goal35% or under

The Challenge

  • 60 minutes of prep, the same for every team no matter how simple or hard the dish is.
  • Then a 1-hour rush to serve 20 customers.
  • Every customer arrives with one ticket. They hand it to your cashier as cash for their food, so customers eat free.
  • Up to 5 staff members.
  • A $140 food budget. You are allowed to go over it, but keep your food cost in mind. Spending more can cost you points unless you raise your plate price to match.
  • You set your own plate price.

How Points Work

Every point you earn is kept and added to your total. Nothing is averaged away. Points come off only when a rule calls for it.

Example: Judges and customers 34.4, health +1, food cost +1, bonus +1 = 37.4 points.

Judges

Each judge scores the restaurant from 1 to 10. Whatever a judge gives goes straight into your total.

Example: Judge 1 gives 9, Judge 2 gives 9 and Judge 3 gives 8. That is 26 points.

Customer Survey

Each of the 20 customers grades their experience at the restaurant based on 5 questions. Those grades from all 20 customers are averaged into one grade that represents the customer score, and that grade goes into your total.

Example:
1. Rate the customer service: 8
2. How quickly your food arrived (1 slow, 10 fast): 9
3. Rate your plate: 8
4. How likely to pay the plate price: 10
5. How likely to return: 7
(8 + 9 + 8 + 10 + 7) ÷ 5 = 8.4 for this customer. The grades from all 20 customers are averaged to get the customer score, 8.4 in this example.

Health Grade Points

The health grade is out of 100. It adds points to your total or takes them off.

A (90 and up)+1
B (80s)+0.5
C (70s)0
D (60s)-0.5
F (below 60)-1

Example: A health grade of 92 is an A, which adds +1 point.

Food Cost Points

Food cost = total spent ÷ (20 plates × plate price). The goal is 35% or under.

35% or under+1
Over 35%, under 70%0
70% or more (double the limit)-1

Judges' plates count. Everything you spend includes the 3 plates you serve the judges, so plan to cook for 23 plates, not 20.

Staying at 35%: plate price × 7 is the most you can spend. A $20 plate allows $140.

Example: Spend $120 on a $20 plate. 20 plates sell for $400, so $120 ÷ $400 = 30%. That earns +1.

Over budget? You are free to spend more than $140. Raise your plate price to match so your food cost stays at 35% or under.

Example: Spend $210 and keep the $20 plate: $210 ÷ $400 = 52.5%, which earns 0 points. Charge $30 instead: $210 ÷ $600 = 35%, which earns +1.

Bonus and Penalty Points

Served all 20 customers in the hour+0.5
Spent $140 or under+0.5
Did not serve all 20 customers-0.5

Going over $140 only means you miss the +0.5 budget bonus. Watch your food cost, because that is where overspending can really cost you.

Service Time is the total time used out of the 1-hour clock. It is recorded for bragging rights and does not change the score. A Service Time of 60:00 means the contestant used the whole hour and was cut off.

The Final Grade

Final score = judges + customer survey + health points + food cost points + bonus and penalty points. Perfect 10s from everyone is 40 points, and bonus points can take you above 40.

A+Above 40
A40 to 36
B35.9 to 32
C31.9 to 28
D27.9 to 24
FBelow 24

Full example: Judges 9 + 9 + 8 = 26, customer survey 8.4, health grade A (+1), food cost 30% (+1), served all 20 customers (+0.5), spent $120 (+0.5). 26 + 8.4 + 1 + 1 + 0.5 + 0.5 = 37.4 points, which is an A.
